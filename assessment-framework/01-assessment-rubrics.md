STANDARDISED ASSESSMENT RUBRICS

1. PURPOSE

This document establishes standardised assessment rubrics for evaluating learner achievement across EduQuest Grades 2–6.

It translates the EduQuest Assessment and Mastery Framework into observable performance criteria that teachers and authorised assessors can apply consistently.

The rubrics shall assess not only correctness but also conceptual understanding, reasoning, practical competence, communication, independence and the ability to transfer learning into unfamiliar situations.

2. CORE ASSESSMENT DIMENSIONS

Significant assessments shall evaluate relevant dimensions from the following framework:

1. Knowledge and recall.
2. Conceptual understanding.
3. Reasoning and problem-solving.
4. Accuracy and procedural competence.
5. Application and transfer.
6. Communication and explanation.
7. Creativity and production.
8. Independence and self-regulation.
9. Reflection and improvement.

Not every assessment requires all nine dimensions. Criteria must correspond to the intended learning outcomes.

3. STANDARD PERFORMANCE SCALE

EduQuest shall use a five-level performance scale.

Level| Classification| Description
1| Beginning| Demonstrates limited understanding and requires substantial assistance.
2| Developing| Demonstrates partial understanding but requires regular guidance.
3| Approaching Mastery| Demonstrates substantial understanding with occasional errors or inconsistent independence.
4| Mastered| Demonstrates accurate, independent and consistent achievement of the intended outcome.
5| Advanced Application| Transfers learning independently to unfamiliar, complex or extended situations.

The performance scale shall be applied against observable evidence rather than subjective impressions.

4. GENERAL ANALYTICAL RUBRIC

4.1 Knowledge and Understanding

Level 1 — Beginning

- Recognises few relevant concepts.
- Demonstrates significant misconceptions.
- Requires substantial explanation and support.

Level 2 — Developing

- Recognises some essential concepts.
- Demonstrates partial understanding.
- Requires regular prompting.

Level 3 — Approaching Mastery

- Understands most essential concepts.
- Explains familiar ideas with reasonable accuracy.
- Occasional misconceptions remain.

Level 4 — Mastered

- Demonstrates secure understanding.
- Explains concepts accurately and independently.
- Distinguishes related ideas appropriately.

Level 5 — Advanced Application

- Integrates concepts across different contexts.
- Explains relationships and implications.
- Demonstrates extended conceptual understanding.

4.2 Reasoning and Problem-Solving

Level 1: Cannot independently identify an appropriate approach.

Level 2: Attempts a suitable approach with substantial assistance.

Level 3: Selects an appropriate approach but demonstrates occasional errors or incomplete reasoning.

Level 4: Selects, applies and explains appropriate methods independently.

Level 5: Evaluates alternative approaches, justifies decisions and solves unfamiliar problems.

4.3 Accuracy and Procedural Competence

Level 1: Demonstrates limited procedural accuracy.

Level 2: Completes some procedural steps correctly with assistance.

Level 3: Completes most steps correctly but remains inconsistent.

Level 4: Performs procedures accurately and independently.

Level 5: Performs accurately and efficiently, identifies errors and adapts methods appropriately.

4.4 Application and Transfer

Level 1: Cannot apply learning beyond direct examples.

Level 2: Applies learning in familiar situations with assistance.

Level 3: Applies learning to familiar situations independently but struggles with unfamiliar variations.

Level 4: Applies learning independently in different contexts.

Level 5: Transfers learning creatively to unfamiliar or complex situations.

4.5 Communication and Explanation

Level 1: Provides incomplete or unclear explanations.

Level 2: Communicates basic ideas with substantial prompting.

Level 3: Provides understandable explanations with some gaps.

Level 4: Communicates clearly, accurately and logically.

Level 5: Presents sophisticated, well-structured explanations appropriate to the task and audience.

Communication shall be assessed in relation to the learner's developmental stage and language of instruction.

4.6 Independence and Self-Regulation

Level 1: Requires continuous adult direction.

Level 2: Completes tasks with frequent reminders and assistance.

Level 3: Completes familiar tasks independently but needs occasional support.

Level 4: Plans and completes tasks independently and responsibly.

Level 5: Demonstrates sustained independent learning, monitors progress and selects appropriate strategies.

4.7 Creativity and Production

Level 1: Produces incomplete work with limited evidence of understanding.

Level 2: Produces basic work following substantial guidance.

Level 3: Produces relevant work with some independent ideas.

Level 4: Produces purposeful, well-developed work demonstrating originality and competence.

Level 5: Produces innovative work, integrates ideas and justifies creative decisions.

4.8 Reflection and Improvement

Level 1: Cannot identify learning difficulties or errors.

Level 2: Identifies difficulties when prompted.

Level 3: Identifies errors and suggests basic improvements.

Level 4: Evaluates work and independently identifies improvement priorities.

Level 5: Uses evidence, feedback and self-evaluation to plan and monitor sustained improvement.

5. SUBJECT-SPECIFIC ASSESSMENT APPLICATION

5.1 Mathematics

Assessment shall consider:

- Numerical understanding.
- Mathematical accuracy.
- Computational fluency.
- Logical reasoning.
- Problem-solving strategies.
- Mathematical communication.
- Practical application.

Evidence may include written solutions, oral explanations, manipulatives, measurements, investigations and real-life mathematical tasks.

Correct answers without sufficient evidence of understanding shall not automatically establish mastery of a reasoning-based outcome.

5.2 English Language

Assessment shall consider:

- Listening comprehension.
- Speaking and oral communication.
- Reading accuracy and fluency.
- Reading comprehension.
- Vocabulary development.
- Grammar and language structures.
- Writing organisation and accuracy.
- Independent communication.

Assessment shall distinguish mechanical accuracy from comprehension and meaningful communication.

5.3 Kiswahili

Assessment shall consider:

- Listening comprehension.
- Oral expression.
- Reading fluency.
- Reading comprehension.
- Vocabulary.
- Sarufi and language structures.
- Written expression.
- Appropriate communication in different contexts.

5.4 Science

Assessment shall consider:

- Scientific knowledge.
- Observation.
- Question formulation.
- Investigation and experimentation.
- Data recording.
- Interpretation of evidence.
- Scientific explanation.
- Practical application.

Learners shall be assessed on the quality of their investigative reasoning, not merely the correctness of a final conclusion.

5.5 Social Studies

Assessment shall consider:

- Understanding of society and environment.
- Historical and geographical knowledge.
- Interpretation of information.
- Evidence-based explanations.
- Civic awareness.
- Understanding of community relationships.
- Responsible application of knowledge.

5.6 Creative and Practical Subjects

Assessment shall consider:

- Understanding of task requirements.
- Practical technique.
- Planning and execution.
- Creativity.
- Quality of production.
- Safe and responsible practice.
- Reflection and improvement.

6. GRADE-LEVEL EXPECTATIONS

Rubric complexity shall increase progressively across Grades 2–6.

Grade 2

- Emphasis on recognition, understanding and concrete demonstration.
- Short explanations and guided tasks.
- Observable foundational competence.

Grade 3

- Increasing independence.
- Clearer oral and written explanations.
- Structured problem-solving.

Grade 4

- More developed reasoning.
- Multi-step tasks.
- Interpretation and explanation of evidence.

Grade 5

- Independent application.
- Analytical thinking.
- Research and project-based evidence.

Grade 6

- Consolidated competence.
- Extended reasoning and communication.
- Independent problem-solving.
- Interdisciplinary transfer.

Grade-level expectations must be validated against applicable TIE curriculum outcomes.

7. SCORING AND INTERPRETATION

Where numerical scoring is appropriate, the five-level rubric may be translated into a percentage score using a clearly defined assessment scheme.

For example:

Performance level| Indicative percentage band
Beginning| 0–39%
Developing| 40–59%
Approaching Mastery| 60–79%
Mastered| 80–94%
Advanced Application| 95–100%

These bands are provisional internal EduQuest reporting conventions, not official TIE grading categories.

They shall not override qualitative evidence.

A learner may achieve a high numerical score but still require further demonstration of conceptual understanding, practical competence or independent application.

Conversely, a practical or creative outcome shall not be reduced to a percentage where a qualitative performance rubric provides more valid evidence.

8. MASTERY VERIFICATION RULES

A learning outcome shall be classified as Mastered only when:

1. The learner demonstrates the essential knowledge or skill.
2. Performance is substantially accurate.
3. The learner can explain or demonstrate understanding where appropriate.
4. The learner performs independently.
5. Evidence is sufficiently consistent across appropriate assessment opportunities.

Advanced Application requires additional evidence of successful transfer or extension.

One correct response shall not automatically establish mastery.

9. ASSESSMENT EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Acceptable evidence may include:

- Written assessment responses.
- Oral explanations.
- Practical demonstrations.
- Recorded investigations.
- Learner-produced models.
- Project reports.
- Research assignments.
- Teacher observation records.
- Digital assessment results.
- Portfolio evidence.

Assessors shall identify the learning outcome being evaluated and record the relevant evidence.

10. INCLUSIVE ASSESSMENT

Assessment shall provide equitable opportunities for learners with different educational needs.

Where appropriate, accommodations may include:

- Additional processing time.
- Accessible learning materials.
- Alternative response formats.
- Oral instead of written responses where the construct permits.
- Appropriate assistive technologies.
- Adjusted presentation of assessment materials.

Accommodations shall preserve the essential learning outcome being assessed.

11. MODERATION AND QUALITY ASSURANCE

EduQuest shall implement:

1. Common assessment criteria.
2. Shared marking guidance.
3. Periodic moderation of teacher-marked work.
4. Review of borderline mastery decisions.
5. Comparison of assessment judgments across classes.
6. Review of assessment validity and difficulty.
7. Documentation of significant assessment decisions.

Where multiple assessors evaluate the same work, moderation shall be used to improve consistency.

12. DIGITAL IMPLEMENTATION REQUIREMENTS

The EduQuest platform should progressively support:

- Rubric selection by subject and learning outcome.
- Five-level performance recording.
- Teacher comments and feedback.
- Evidence attachment.
- Mastery status tracking.
- Assessment history.
- Remediation recommendations.
- Portfolio integration.
- Learner progress reporting.

Automated scoring may support objective assessment but shall not independently determine mastery in complex performance-based tasks.

13. GOVERNANCE

All subject-specific assessment instruments shall comply with this standard.

Where a subject requires additional specialised criteria, those criteria shall supplement rather than contradict the general assessment framework.

The Assessment and Mastery Framework (EQ-AMF-001) shall remain the governing policy document.

14. REVIEW AND VALIDATION

This document shall be reviewed following:

- Development of grade-specific assessment instruments.
- Pilot implementation.
- Teacher moderation.
- Analysis of learner performance.
- Curriculum alignment verification.
- Assessment validation.
