1. PURPOSE

This document establishes the institutional requirements governing the frequency, scheduling, distribution and developmental progression of assessments across Grades 2–6.

It ensures that assessment is systematic, continuous, developmentally appropriate and directly connected to curriculum learning outcomes.

The framework shall:

1. Establish consistent assessment cycles across all grades.
2. Ensure regular monitoring of learner understanding and progression.
3. Identify misconceptions and learning gaps before they become persistent.
4. Provide opportunities for learners to demonstrate knowledge and competence through multiple assessment methods.
5. Support evidence-based teaching adjustments and targeted interventions.
6. Establish reliable records of learner achievement and mastery.
7. Balance assessment demands with effective teaching and learning time.
8. Support institutional monitoring and quality assurance.

Assessment frequency must serve learning improvement rather than create unnecessary testing.

2. CORE ASSESSMENT PRINCIPLES

2.1 Continuity

Assessment shall be integrated throughout teaching and learning rather than restricted to examinations.

2.2 Purposeful Frequency

Every assessment activity must have a defined educational purpose and contribute useful evidence.

2.3 Progressive Complexity

Assessment expectations shall increase from Grade 2 to Grade 6 in accordance with learner development and curriculum progression.

2.4 Multiple Evidence

Mastery decisions must draw on different assessment activities and evidence collected over time.

2.5 Timely Intervention

Assessment findings must lead to appropriate instructional responses, remediation or enrichment.

2.6 Balanced Assessment

Assessment scheduling must preserve sufficient time for instruction, guided practice, independent learning and practical activities.

3. STANDARD ASSESSMENT CYCLE

EduQuest shall implement the following assessment cycle.

3.1 Daily Formative Assessment

Purpose: Monitor immediate understanding during instruction.

Examples:

- Oral questioning.
- Short written exercises.
- Mental mathematics.
- Reading checks.
- Practical demonstrations.
- Observation of learner participation.
- Exit questions.
- Short digital knowledge checks.

Expected outcome: Immediate identification of understanding, misconceptions and areas requiring clarification.

3.2 Weekly Knowledge Checks

Purpose: Evaluate understanding of the week's principal learning outcomes.

Examples:

- Short subject quizzes.
- Reading comprehension.
- Mathematical problem-solving.
- Vocabulary assessments.
- Scientific concept checks.
- Short written explanations.

Expected outcome: Identification of learning gaps and adjustment of subsequent instruction.

3.3 Unit Assessments

Purpose: Evaluate achievement after completion of a defined learning unit.

Examples:

- Structured written assessments.
- Practical competency tasks.
- Problem-solving exercises.
- Investigations.
- Short projects.
- Oral presentations.

Expected outcome: Evidence of achievement against the unit's intended learning outcomes.

3.4 Monthly Progress Reviews

Purpose: Examine learner progress across completed learning units.

Reviews should consider:

- Assessment records.
- Competency development.
- Mastery status.
- Persistent misconceptions.
- Practical performance.
- Reading and mathematical progression.
- Participation and learning independence.

Expected outcome: A documented learner progress review and recommended instructional response.

3.5 Mid-Term or Mid-Cycle Review

Purpose: Identify progress patterns and potential learning difficulties before the end of the term.

The review should examine:

- Achievement against planned learning outcomes.
- Progress across subjects.
- Areas of sustained difficulty.
- Retention of previously taught content.
- Effectiveness of interventions.

Expected outcome: A documented adjustment to teaching, learner support or enrichment arrangements where necessary.

3.6 End-of-Term Assessment

Purpose: Evaluate achievement across the curriculum outcomes taught during the term.

Appropriate methods may include:

- Written assessments.
- Oral assessments.
- Practical examinations.
- Problem-solving tasks.
- Projects.
- Portfolio reviews.

Expected outcome: A term achievement profile supported by recorded assessment evidence.

3.7 End-of-Year Assessment

Purpose: Evaluate cumulative achievement, retention, application and readiness for progression.

The assessment should establish:

- Achievement of annual learning outcomes.
- Strength of foundational competencies.
- Retention of essential knowledge.
- Ability to apply learning.
- Areas requiring continued support.
- Readiness for the next grade.

Expected outcome: An annual learner achievement and progression report.

5. SUBJECT-LEVEL ASSESSMENT DISTRIBUTION

Assessment activities must reflect the nature of each subject.

5.1 Mathematics

Assessment should regularly cover:

- Number knowledge and operations.
- Mathematical reasoning.
- Patterns and relationships.
- Measurement.
- Geometry.
- Data interpretation.
- Real-world problem-solving.

Weekly checks should include both procedural accuracy and mathematical reasoning.

Unit assessments should incorporate problems requiring learners to select and explain appropriate methods.

5.2 English Language

Assessment should regularly cover:

- Listening.
- Speaking.
- Reading fluency.
- Reading comprehension.
- Vocabulary.
- Grammar.
- Writing.

Reading and writing progression must be monitored throughout the academic year.

5.3 Kiswahili

Assessment should regularly cover:

- Kusikiliza.
- Kuzungumza.
- Kusoma.
- Ufahamu.
- Msamiati.
- Sarufi.
- Uandishi.

Assessment should evaluate effective language use and communication in relevant contexts.

5.4 Science

Assessment should regularly cover:

- Scientific knowledge.
- Observation.
- Classification.
- Prediction.
- Investigation.
- Evidence recording.
- Interpretation.
- Practical application.

Practical assessment should be incorporated into relevant learning units rather than reserved exclusively for end-of-term examinations.

5.5 Social Studies

Assessment should regularly cover:

- Historical understanding.
- Geography.
- Civic knowledge.
- Environmental awareness.
- Map interpretation.
- Community understanding.
- Evidence-based explanation.

5.6 Creative Arts and Practical Learning

Assessment should be incorporated throughout practical activities.

Learners should be evaluated on:

- Planning.
- Creativity.
- Technical execution.
- Appropriate material selection.
- Completion.
- Improvement.
- Reflection.

6. ASSESSMENT PROGRESSION ACROSS THE ACADEMIC YEAR

The academic year shall be organised into planned teaching, assessment and intervention cycles.

The following model provides a standard implementation structure. Actual dates and teaching periods must follow the approved institutional calendar and applicable Tanzanian requirements.

Phase 1: Initial Diagnostic Review

At the beginning of the academic year:

- Establish prior knowledge.
- Identify foundational learning gaps.
- Review previous achievement records.
- Determine learner support requirements.
- Establish initial competency profiles.

Phase 2: Teaching and Continuous Monitoring

Throughout each term:

- Conduct daily formative assessment.
- Administer weekly knowledge checks.
- Complete unit assessments.
- Record learner performance.
- Provide timely feedback.

Phase 3: Progress Evaluation

At planned intervals:

- Conduct monthly progress reviews.
- Analyse subject-level performance.
- Identify learners requiring intervention.
- Review effectiveness of teaching strategies.

Phase 4: Consolidated Achievement Evaluation

At the end of each term:

- Conduct appropriate subject assessments.
- Review practical and project evidence.
- Evaluate achievement against intended outcomes.
- Produce learner progress reports.

Phase 5: Annual Mastery and Progression Review

At the end of the academic year:

- Review cumulative achievement.
- Verify essential competencies.
- Evaluate retention and application.
- Identify outstanding learning gaps.
- Determine readiness for the next grade.
- Prepare individual progression recommendations.

7. INTERVENTION AND REMEDIATION CYCLE

Assessment must result in appropriate educational action.

Where a learner demonstrates insufficient understanding, the teacher shall:

1. Identify the specific competency not yet achieved.
2. Examine the assessment evidence.
3. Determine the likely source of difficulty.
4. Select an appropriate instructional intervention.
5. Provide targeted teaching and guided practice.
6. Reassess the identified competency.
7. Record the learner's response to intervention.
8. Adjust the support strategy where necessary.

Intervention should be competency-specific rather than based solely on a learner's overall subject score.

8. ENRICHMENT AND ADVANCED LEARNING

Learners who consistently demonstrate mastery should receive opportunities for deeper learning.

Enrichment may include:

- Advanced problem-solving.
- Independent investigations.
- Extended reading.
- Research activities.
- Interdisciplinary projects.
- Creative design challenges.
- Peer explanation and knowledge-sharing activities.
- Application to unfamiliar contexts.

Enrichment must extend intellectual challenge rather than simply increase repetitive workload.

9. ASSESSMENT WORKLOAD MANAGEMENT

Assessment must be proportionate to available teaching and learning time.

The institution shall ensure that:

1. Assessments are distributed appropriately across subjects.
2. Major assessments do not create unnecessary scheduling conflicts.
3. Weekly knowledge checks remain manageable.
4. Practical learning is not displaced by excessive written testing.
5. Teachers have sufficient time to mark and interpret evidence.
6. Learners receive feedback within a reasonable period.
7. Assessment activities contribute directly to learning improvement.

A central assessment calendar should be maintained by the assessment coordinator.

10. FEEDBACK AND REPORTING FREQUENCY

Feedback must be timely, specific and actionable.

Assessment stage| Required feedback
Daily formative assessment| Immediate or during the learning activity
Weekly knowledge checks| Within the following instructional cycle
Unit assessment| After marking and review
Monthly progress review| Documented progress and support recommendations
Mid-term review| Consolidated progress feedback
End-of-term assessment| Formal achievement report
End-of-year assessment| Annual achievement and progression report

Feedback should communicate:

- What the learner has achieved.
- What remains to be developed.
- What the learner should do next.
- What instructional support will be provided.

11. MASTERY VERIFICATION AND RETESTING

The assessment cycle shall operate in conjunction with the EduQuest Assessment and Mastery Framework.

The five institutional classifications are:

1. Beginning.
2. Developing.
3. Approaching Mastery.
4. Mastered.
5. Advanced Application.

A single assessment result shall not automatically establish permanent mastery.

Where appropriate, verification should include:

- A second assessment opportunity.
- Application in a different context.
- Independent demonstration.
- Evidence collected over time.
- Retention checks.

Learners should be given reasonable opportunities to demonstrate improvement after intervention.

The EduQuest digital platform should support:

1. Annual assessment planning.
2. Term assessment scheduling.
3. Weekly knowledge-check scheduling.
4. Unit assessment records.
5. Monthly progress reviews.
6. Learner assessment histories.
7. Intervention tracking.
8. Mastery verification.
9. Teacher feedback records.
10. Parent progress reporting.

The platform should generate alerts for overdue assessments and outstanding intervention reviews.

Digital scheduling must remain configurable to accommodate different school calendars and instructional arrangements.

13. INSTITUTIONAL QUALITY ASSURANCE

Assessment coordinators and curriculum leadership shall periodically review:

- Compliance with the approved assessment calendar.
- Coverage of intended learning outcomes.
- Assessment workload.
- Quality of marking.
- Timeliness of feedback.
- Effectiveness of intervention.
- Learner progression patterns.
- Consistency of mastery judgements.

Assessment frequency should be reviewed using evidence of educational value rather than administrative convenience.

14. NATIONAL AND INTERNATIONAL ALIGNMENT

This framework shall operate in alignment with applicable Tanzanian Ministry of Education requirements and relevant Tanzania Institute of Education curriculum and assessment guidance.

Its approach also reflects internationally recognised principles of:

- Formative assessment.
- Assessment for learning.
- Continuous competency development.
- Inclusive assessment.
- Evidence-based intervention.
- Developmental progression.
- Constructive alignment.

International principles shall support quality enhancement without superseding national requirements.

15. GOVERNANCE AND RESPONSIBILITY

Curriculum Leadership: Approves assessment architecture and monitors curriculum alignment.

Subject Specialists: Develop subject-specific assessment schedules and instruments.

Teachers: Conduct assessments, record evidence, provide feedback and implement interventions.

Assessment Coordinators: Maintain assessment calendars, monitor completion and coordinate moderation.

Digital Platform Administrators: Maintain assessment scheduling, data integrity and secure learner records.

Institutional Leadership: Reviews assessment effectiveness and directs programme improvement.
