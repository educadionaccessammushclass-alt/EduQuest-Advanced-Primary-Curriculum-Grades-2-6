08. ACADEMIC APPEALS AND ASSESSMENT INTEGRITY FRAMEWORK

1. Purpose

This framework establishes institutional procedures for protecting academic integrity, ensuring fair assessment practices, managing academic misconduct and providing transparent mechanisms through which learners and parents may challenge assessment decisions.

It applies across Grades 2–6 of the EduQuest Advanced Primary Curriculum.

The framework seeks to ensure that all assessment decisions are credible, impartial, evidence-based and consistent with the Academy's academic standards.

2. Guiding Principles

The framework shall be governed by the following principles:

1. Academic honesty and personal responsibility.
2. Fairness and impartiality.
3. Transparency in assessment decisions.
4. Consistent application of academic standards.
5. Protection of learner rights and dignity.
6. Confidential handling of complaints.
7. Proportionate responses to academic misconduct.
8. Opportunities for correction, learning and improvement.
9. Protection against discrimination and unfair treatment.
10. Accountability of teachers, assessors and institutional leadership.

3. Scope of Academic Integrity

Academic integrity applies to all assessment activities, including:

- Written examinations.
- Classroom tests.
- Homework and assignments.
- Research projects.
- Practical assessments.
- Oral presentations.
- Group projects.
- Digital assessments.
- Portfolio submissions.
- Independent learning activities.

All learners shall be taught age-appropriate expectations concerning honesty, originality, attribution and responsible use of learning resources.

4. Academic Misconduct

Academic misconduct refers to behaviour that compromises the fairness, authenticity or reliability of an assessment.

Examples may include:

4.1 Cheating

- Copying another learner's answers during an assessment.
- Using unauthorised materials.
- Receiving prohibited assistance.
- Accessing unauthorised information during examinations.

4.2 Plagiarism

- Presenting another person's work as one's own.
- Copying written material without appropriate acknowledgement.
- Submitting work prepared entirely by another person.

4.3 Fabrication

- Inventing research findings.
- Providing deliberately false information in an assignment.
- Falsifying assessment records or evidence.

4.4 Unauthorised Collaboration

- Working with others where independent work is required.
- Sharing answers in restricted assessments.
- Submitting jointly produced work as individual work without permission.

4.5 Digital Assessment Misconduct

- Accessing prohibited online resources.
- Using another learner's account.
- Manipulating digital assessment submissions.
- Attempting to alter assessment results.
- Using unauthorised artificial intelligence tools where their use is expressly prohibited.

Digital tools and artificial intelligence may be permitted for approved learning activities. Their use shall be governed by clear, age-appropriate instructions.

5. Prevention and Academic Integrity Education

The Academy shall prioritise prevention through education rather than relying exclusively on punishment.

Teachers shall:

- Explain assessment rules before activities begin.
- Clarify which resources may be used.
- Teach learners how to acknowledge sources.
- Promote independent thinking.
- Explain the importance of original work.
- Provide appropriate supervision.
- Design assessments that encourage genuine understanding.

Learners shall receive guidance appropriate to their age and developmental stage.

6. Assessment Security

The Academy shall establish reasonable safeguards to protect assessment materials and results.

These shall include:

- Controlled access to examination papers.
- Secure storage of assessment instruments.
- Appropriate examination supervision.
- Protection of digital assessment accounts.
- Restricted access to confidential marking schemes.
- Controlled modification of assessment results.
- Documented procedures for handling examination materials.

Assessment records shall not be altered without authorised justification and an appropriate audit trail.

7. Academic Appeals

Learners and parents or guardians shall have the right to request a review of an assessment decision.

Grounds for an appeal may include:

1. Suspected marking errors.
2. Incorrect calculation of marks.
3. Failure to apply published assessment criteria.
4. Procedural irregularities.
5. Failure to consider approved learning accommodations.
6. Administrative errors affecting assessment results.
7. Evidence of inconsistent or unfair assessment treatment.

Disagreement with an academic result alone shall not automatically establish that an assessment decision was incorrect.

8. Academic Appeals Procedure

The following procedure shall apply:

Stage 1: Initial Clarification

The learner or parent may request clarification from the relevant teacher.

The teacher shall explain the assessment criteria, marking decision and relevant feedback.

Stage 2: Formal Appeal

Where the matter remains unresolved, a written appeal may be submitted to the Academic Coordinator.

The appeal shall identify:

- Learner and grade.
- Subject and assessment concerned.
- Date of assessment.
- Nature of the concern.
- Grounds for requesting a review.
- Relevant supporting information.

Stage 3: Independent Review

The Academic Coordinator shall arrange an impartial review of the assessment.

Where appropriate, another qualified teacher or assessor shall examine the relevant work and marking criteria.

Stage 4: Institutional Decision

The Academic Coordinator or authorised school leadership shall communicate the decision.

Possible outcomes include:

- Confirmation of the original result.
- Correction of an identified marking error.
- Reassessment where justified.
- Review of an identified procedural irregularity.
- Recommendation for further academic support.

Stage 5: Further Escalation

Where the matter remains unresolved, it may be referred to the Head of the Academy or an authorised institutional review body.

All decisions shall be documented.

9. Indicative Appeal Timelines

The Academy shall establish clear administrative timelines.

The recommended internal standards are:

- Initial clarification: within five working days.
- Formal appeal submission: within ten working days of receiving the result.
- Independent review: within ten working days of receiving a complete appeal.
- Communication of the final institutional decision: within five working days after the review.

Where exceptional circumstances require additional time, the appellant shall be informed of the reason and expected completion date.

10. Management of Academic Misconduct

Suspected misconduct shall be investigated fairly before disciplinary action is taken.

The process shall include:

1. Identification of the suspected incident.
2. Collection of relevant evidence.
3. Confidential documentation.
4. Opportunity for the learner to explain what occurred.
5. Consideration of the learner's age and understanding.
6. Determination based on available evidence.
7. Proportionate corrective or disciplinary action.
8. Communication with parents or guardians where appropriate.

A learner shall not be presumed guilty solely on the basis of an allegation.

11. Corrective and Disciplinary Measures

Responses shall be educational, proportionate and developmentally appropriate.

Possible measures include:

- Guidance and counselling.
- Re-teaching academic integrity expectations.
- Repeating an assessment under appropriate supervision.
- Resubmission of work where permitted.
- Partial or full invalidation of affected assessment evidence where justified.
- Parent or guardian engagement.
- Further institutional disciplinary procedures in serious or repeated cases.

The Academy shall distinguish between deliberate misconduct, misunderstanding, accidental procedural errors and difficulties arising from inadequate instruction.

12. Protection of Learners During Appeals

Throughout the appeals process:

- Learners shall be treated respectfully.
- Complaints shall be handled confidentially.
- Retaliation against learners making legitimate appeals shall not be permitted.
- Decisions shall be based on evidence.
- Reasonable accommodations shall be considered.
- Parents or guardians may participate where appropriate.
- Learners shall receive explanations they can understand.

No learner shall be academically disadvantaged merely for exercising the right to request a legitimate review.

13. Responsibilities

Learners

- Follow assessment instructions.
- Submit authentic work.
- Respect examination procedures.
- Report suspected assessment irregularities.

Teachers and Assessors

- Apply assessment criteria consistently.
- Maintain accurate records.
- Explain marking decisions.
- Protect assessment materials.
- Report suspected misconduct appropriately.

Academic Coordinators

- Receive and manage formal appeals.
- Coordinate independent reviews.
- Investigate assessment irregularities.
- Maintain appeal records.
- Recommend improvements to assessment practices.

School Leadership

- Ensure procedural fairness.
- Authorise institutional decisions.
- Protect learner rights.
- Monitor assessment integrity.
- Review institutional compliance.

Parents and Guardians

- Support academic honesty.
- Encourage responsible learning.
- Participate constructively in appeal procedures.
- Respect established institutional processes.

14. Records and Documentation

The Academy shall maintain appropriate records of:

- Formal academic appeals.
- Assessment reviews.
- Marking corrections.
- Suspected misconduct incidents.
- Investigation findings.
- Institutional decisions.
- Corrective measures.
- Repeated assessment irregularities.

Records shall be protected through appropriate confidentiality and access controls.

15. Quality Assurance and Policy Review

The effectiveness of this framework shall be evaluated through:

- Number and nature of academic appeals.
- Frequency of confirmed marking errors.
- Assessment irregularity reports.
- Consistency of assessment decisions.
- Timeliness of appeal resolution.
- Compliance with assessment security procedures.
- Effectiveness of academic integrity education.

Findings shall inform improvements in assessment design, teacher training and institutional procedures.

16. Alignment with Educational Standards

The framework shall operate in accordance with applicable Tanzanian education requirements and institutional assessment policies.

It shall reflect recognised principles of:

- Fair assessment.
- Academic honesty.
- Learner protection.
- Inclusive education.
- Transparent assessment procedures.
- Educational accountability.

The framework shall complement, rather than replace, applicable national laws, regulations and Ministry of Education requirements.

17. Expected Outcomes

Effective implementation shall achieve:

1. Greater confidence in assessment results.
2. Reduced academic misconduct.
3. More consistent marking practices.
4. Transparent academic appeals.
5. Improved learner understanding of academic honesty.
6. Stronger assessment security.
7. Fairer institutional decision-making.
8. Improved accountability among teachers and assessors.
9. Greater trust among learners, parents and the Academy.
