1. PURPOSE

This document establishes the institutional standards governing the marking, scoring, interpretation, moderation and quality assurance of learner assessments across Grades 2–6.

It ensures that assessment judgements are accurate, transparent, consistent, fair and aligned with the intended curriculum competencies.

The standard shall:

1. Establish consistent marking procedures across subjects and grade levels.
2. Define expectations for objective and constructed-response marking.
3. Establish common requirements for assessment rubrics.
4. Ensure that partial understanding and relevant reasoning receive appropriate recognition.
5. Improve consistency between teachers and assessors.
6. Strengthen the reliability of practical and project-based assessment.
7. Establish procedures for internal moderation.
8. Reduce subjective bias in assessment decisions.
9. Ensure that assessment results provide dependable evidence of learner achievement.
10. Support institutional quality assurance and continuous improvement.

Marking must measure demonstrated learning rather than merely reward correct answers or penalise mistakes.

2. FOUNDATIONAL MARKING PRINCIPLES

2.1 Accuracy

Marks must reflect the quality and correctness of the learner's demonstrated knowledge and competence.

2.2 Consistency

Equivalent responses and performances must receive equivalent assessment judgements under the same marking criteria.

2.3 Transparency

Marking criteria must be sufficiently clear to allow teachers, learners and relevant stakeholders to understand assessment decisions.

2.4 Curriculum Alignment

Marks must relate directly to the intended learning outcomes and competencies.

2.5 Recognition of Reasoning

Where reasoning is part of the assessed competency, appropriate credit must be given for valid methods, explanations and logical processes.

2.6 Developmental Appropriateness

Expectations must reflect the learner's grade, developmental stage and level of instruction.

2.7 Constructive Feedback

Marking must contribute to improved learning by identifying achievement, misconceptions and next steps.

2.8 Professional Judgement

Automated scoring and standardised marking guides shall support, but not unnecessarily replace, professional teacher judgement.

3. MARKING CATEGORIES

EduQuest shall recognise the following marking categories.

3.1 Objective Marking

Applicable to:

- Multiple-choice questions.
- True or false questions.
- Matching exercises.
- Classification tasks.
- Short factual responses.
- Clearly defined identification questions.

Requirements:

- Correct answers must be predetermined.
- Mark allocation must be explicit.
- Acceptable alternative responses must be identified where appropriate.
- Marking must follow consistent rules.

3.2 Constructed-Response Marking

Applicable to:

- Explanations.
- Short-answer questions.
- Mathematical working.
- Paragraphs.
- Comprehension responses.
- Analytical writing.

Requirements:

- Marking guides must identify expected knowledge and reasoning.
- Relevant alternative answers must be considered.
- Partial credit must be awarded where justified.
- Language errors should only affect marks where language accuracy is an intended assessment objective.

3.3 Practical Performance Marking

Applicable to:

- Scientific experiments.
- Mathematical measurement.
- Construction activities.
- Demonstrations.
- Creative arts.
- Technical procedures.

Requirements:

- Performance criteria must be observable.
- Scoring must distinguish procedure, accuracy and safe execution where relevant.
- Assessment must consider the process as well as the final outcome.

3.4 Project and Portfolio Marking

Applicable to:

- Research assignments.
- Integrated projects.
- Creative products.
- Investigations.
- Extended learning tasks.
- Learner portfolios.

Requirements:

- Assessment criteria must be established before the task.
- The process and final product must receive appropriate consideration.
- Individual contribution must be identifiable in collaborative projects.
- Reflection and improvement should be assessed where relevant.

3.5 Oral Assessment Marking

Applicable to:

- Speaking exercises.
- Presentations.
- Oral reading.
- Verbal explanations.
- Discussions.
- Listening and response activities.

Requirements:

- Criteria must reflect the competency being assessed.
- Pronunciation, fluency, comprehension and reasoning should be distinguished where relevant.
- Accent differences must not be penalised unless a specific pronunciation standard is an intended learning outcome.

4. STANDARD MARK ALLOCATION

Every formal assessment instrument must include a marking structure established before administration.

The marking structure should specify:

1. Total available marks.
2. Marks allocated to each question or task.
3. Marks allocated to individual assessment criteria.
4. Acceptable answers and alternatives.
5. Partial-credit provisions.
6. Conditions for awarding full or zero marks.
7. Any relevant performance descriptors.

Marks should reflect the relative importance and complexity of the assessed learning outcomes.

A question assessing several competencies may allocate marks separately for each competency.

The same competency should not be unintentionally rewarded or penalised multiple times within one assessment.

5. PARTIAL-CREDIT POLICY

Partial credit shall be awarded where a learner demonstrates relevant understanding or valid reasoning despite an incomplete or incorrect final response.

5.1 Mathematics

Where appropriate, marking should distinguish:

- Selection of an appropriate method.
- Correct mathematical procedure.
- Accuracy of calculation.
- Final answer.
- Interpretation of the result.

A learner who selects an appropriate method but makes a minor computational error may receive credit for the valid mathematical reasoning.

Repeated consequential errors should not automatically result in repeated penalties where the subsequent method is otherwise correct.

5.2 English Language

Marking should distinguish:

- Understanding of the question.
- Relevance of the response.
- Organisation of ideas.
- Vocabulary.
- Grammar.
- Spelling and punctuation.

Language accuracy should be weighted according to the purpose of the task.

5.3 Kiswahili

Marking should distinguish:

- Ufahamu.
- Usahihi wa majibu.
- Matumizi ya msamiati.
- Sarufi.
- Mpangilio wa mawazo.
- Ufasaha wa mawasiliano.

5.4 Science

Marking should recognise:

- Correct observation.
- Relevant prediction.
- Appropriate investigation procedure.
- Accurate recording.
- Interpretation of findings.
- Evidence-based explanation.

5.5 Social Studies

Marking should recognise:

- Factual accuracy.
- Interpretation of information.
- Understanding of relationships.
- Appropriate use of evidence.
- Logical explanation.
- Relevant application.

6. ASSESSMENT RUBRIC STANDARD

EduQuest shall use structured rubrics for assessments involving explanation, reasoning, performance, projects and other tasks that require professional judgement.

Every formal rubric should contain:

1. Assessment title.
2. Grade and subject.
3. Learning outcomes.
4. Assessment criteria.
5. Performance levels.
6. Descriptions of observable achievement.
7. Mark allocation or scoring guidance.
8. Instructions for assessor interpretation.

6.1 Standard Performance Levels

The following institutional performance levels shall be used:

Level| Classification| General description
1| Beginning| Demonstrates limited understanding and requires substantial support.
2| Developing| Demonstrates emerging understanding but requires continued guidance.
3| Approaching Mastery| Demonstrates substantial understanding with some remaining inconsistencies.
4| Mastered| Demonstrates secure and consistent achievement of the intended competency.
5| Advanced Application| Demonstrates secure mastery and transfers learning to appropriately challenging situations.

These classifications are criterion-referenced descriptions, not automatic percentage bands.

The exact descriptors must be adapted to the subject, grade and assessed competency.

6.2 Analytic Rubrics

Analytic rubrics should be used where different dimensions of performance need separate assessment.

For example, a scientific investigation may be assessed through:

- Question formulation.
- Prediction.
- Procedure.
- Observation.
- Recording.
- Interpretation.
- Conclusion.

Each dimension receives an independent judgement.

6.3 Holistic Rubrics

Holistic rubrics may be used where the overall quality of a performance is more meaningful than separate component scores.

Examples include:

- Oral presentations.
- Creative performances.
- Integrated communication tasks.
- Overall project coherence.

The rubric must still describe distinguishable performance levels.

7. SUBJECT-SPECIFIC MARKING REQUIREMENTS

7.1 Mathematics

Mathematical marking must recognise both accuracy and reasoning.

Teachers should assess:

- Conceptual understanding.
- Appropriate method selection.
- Mathematical procedures.
- Calculation accuracy.
- Problem interpretation.
- Mathematical communication.

Where the assessment specifically measures mental calculation, the marking criteria should reflect that purpose.

7.2 English Language

English marking must distinguish receptive and productive language competencies.

Reading assessments should evaluate comprehension, interpretation and evidence from the text.

Writing assessments should consider:

- Content.
- Organisation.
- Vocabulary.
- Grammar.
- Spelling.
- Punctuation.
- Audience and purpose.

7.3 Kiswahili

Kiswahili marking must recognise appropriate language use and effective communication.

Assessment should consider:

- Ufahamu.
- Matumizi sahihi ya lugha.
- Msamiati.
- Sarufi.
- Muundo wa sentensi.
- Uandishi.
- Uwezo wa kujieleza.

7.4 Science

Science marking should recognise evidence-based thinking.

Teachers must distinguish:

- Observation from inference.
- Prediction from conclusion.
- Procedure from result.
- Evidence from unsupported explanation.

Practical assessment must reward accurate scientific processes, not simply successful outcomes.

7.5 Social Studies

Social Studies marking should recognise:

- Factual knowledge.
- Chronological understanding.
- Geographical interpretation.
- Civic understanding.
- Source interpretation.
- Evidence-based explanation.

Alternative explanations may receive credit where they are relevant, logically supported and consistent with reliable evidence.

7.6 Creative Arts and Practical Learning

Marking must consider:

- Planning.
- Creativity.
- Technical competence.
- Appropriate execution.
- Material selection.
- Completion.
- Reflection and improvement.

Learners must not be disadvantaged because they lack access to expensive materials or specialised equipment.

8. TEACHER MARKING PROCEDURES

Teachers shall follow a standard marking process.

Step 1: Review the assessment instrument

Confirm the learning outcomes, instructions, questions and marking guide.

Step 2: Review the marking criteria

Understand the expected responses, mark allocations and partial-credit provisions.

Step 3: Mark consistently

Apply the same criteria across all learner scripts and performances.

Step 4: Record results accurately

Ensure that awarded marks are correctly transferred into the learner's assessment record.

Step 5: Review unusual responses

Refer ambiguous, unexpected or potentially valid alternative responses to the appropriate subject specialist.

Step 6: Provide feedback

Identify strengths, misconceptions and areas requiring improvement.

Step 7: Recommend intervention

Where necessary, identify competencies requiring further instruction and reassessment.

9. INTERNAL MODERATION

Moderation is the process of reviewing assessment instruments, marking decisions and performance judgements to improve consistency and reliability.

EduQuest shall implement moderation at three levels.

9.1 Pre-Assessment Moderation

Conducted before an assessment is administered.

Review:

- Curriculum alignment.
- Question accuracy.
- Clarity of instructions.
- Grade appropriateness.
- Cognitive demand.
- Mark allocation.
- Rubric adequacy.
- Accessibility.

9.2 During-Marking Moderation

Conducted while assessment is being marked.

Review:

- Interpretation of marking criteria.
- Consistency of partial credit.
- Treatment of alternative answers.
- Accuracy of mark allocation.
- Consistency across learner scripts.

9.3 Post-Assessment Moderation

Conducted after marking is completed.

Review:

- Selected marked scripts.
- Performance patterns.
- Unexpected score distributions.
- Consistency of judgements.
- Recording accuracy.
- Evidence supporting mastery classifications.

Moderation must be educationally constructive and must not become a purely administrative exercise.

10. INTER-MARKER RELIABILITY

Where multiple teachers assess equivalent work, EduQuest should establish procedures for checking consistency.

These may include:

1. Joint review of marking guides.
2. Independent marking of selected sample scripts.
3. Comparison of scoring decisions.
4. Discussion of differences.
5. Agreement on acceptable interpretations.
6. Revision or clarification of marking criteria.
7. Documentation of moderation outcomes.

Where significant disagreement occurs, the relevant assessment coordinator or subject specialist shall facilitate resolution.

A shared marking standard must be established before final assessment results are confirmed.

11. MARKING QUALITY CONTROL

The institution shall monitor:

- Correct application of mark allocations.
- Arithmetic accuracy in total scores.
- Consistency of partial credit.
- Completeness of marking.
- Clarity of written feedback.
- Appropriate treatment of alternative responses.
- Alignment with the approved rubric.
- Accuracy of digital score entry.

Corrections to recorded marks must be traceable.

No assessment result should be changed without a documented educational or administrative justification.

12. FEEDBACK STANDARD

Feedback must be specific, constructive and linked to learning outcomes.

It should identify:

Achievement: What the learner has demonstrated successfully.

Development need: What remains inaccurate, incomplete or insufficiently understood.

Next step: What the learner should do to improve.

Support: What teaching, practice or additional resources will be provided.

Feedback should be age-appropriate and understandable.

For younger learners, teachers may use oral explanations, visual indicators and guided correction.

For older learners, feedback should increasingly support independent reflection and self-correction.

13. LEARNER SELF-ASSESSMENT AND PEER ASSESSMENT

EduQuest shall progressively develop learner participation in assessment.

Learners may be guided to:

- Review their own work against clear criteria.
- Identify errors.
- Explain corrections.
- Reflect on progress.
- Set learning targets.
- Provide structured peer feedback.

Peer assessment must remain supervised by the teacher.

It must not replace professional assessment or expose learners to inappropriate public ranking or humiliation.

14. MARKING AND INCLUSIVE ASSESSMENT

Marking must distinguish between the intended competency and barriers unrelated to that competency.

Teachers should consider approved accessibility arrangements.

Examples:

- Alternative response formats.
- Enlarged print.
- Assistive technology.
- Additional processing time.
- Accessible practical materials.

An adjustment must not remove the central competency being assessed.

Where a learner's language, physical or learning needs require an approved alternative assessment method, the marking criteria must remain aligned with the intended learning outcome.

15. DIGITAL MARKING AND SCORING

The EduQuest digital platform should support:

1. Configurable marking guides.
2. Question-level mark allocation.
3. Automated scoring of suitable objective questions.
4. Teacher marking of constructed responses.
5. Analytic and holistic rubrics.
6. Practical assessment records.
7. Moderation records.
8. Score correction history.
9. Feedback management.
10. Mastery classification.
11. Learner assessment histories.
12. Assessment analytics.
13. Controlled access to learner records.

Automated scoring must not be used where responses require interpretation beyond the system's validated capabilities.

Teachers must be able to review and correct automated results where appropriate, with changes recorded.

16. ASSESSMENT INTEGRITY

All assessment activities must protect the integrity of learner achievement records.

The institution shall establish procedures addressing:

- Unauthorised assistance.
- Plagiarism.
- Misrepresentation of individual work.
- Unauthorised alteration of marks.
- Loss or manipulation of assessment records.
- Inappropriate disclosure of learner results.

Any suspected assessment irregularity must be investigated fairly and proportionately.

Learners should be given an age-appropriate opportunity to explain their work.

17. MODERATION RECORDS

Every formal moderation activity should produce a record containing:

1. Assessment identification.
2. Grade and subject.
3. Assessment date.
4. Moderator's identity and role.
5. Instruments or scripts reviewed.
6. Findings.
7. Identified inconsistencies.
8. Corrective actions.
9. Responsible personnel.
10. Completion status.

Moderation records shall be retained in accordance with institutional records-management and applicable data-protection requirements.

18. NATIONAL AND INTERNATIONAL ALIGNMENT

This standard shall operate in alignment with applicable Tanzanian Ministry of Education requirements and relevant Tanzania Institute of Education curriculum and assessment guidance.

It also draws on recognised international principles of:

- Criterion-referenced assessment.
- Assessment for learning.
- Assessment reliability.
- Validity.
- Transparent performance criteria.
- Inclusive assessment.
- Professional moderation.
- Evidence-based evaluation.

These principles support quality enhancement and do not supersede applicable national requirements.

No claim of formal national approval, accreditation or equivalence shall be made without appropriate official evidence.

19. GOVERNANCE AND RESPONSIBILITY

Curriculum Leadership: Approves institutional marking standards and ensures curriculum alignment.

Subject Specialists: Develop marking guides, rubrics and subject-specific scoring criteria.

Teachers: Mark assessments, record results, provide feedback and maintain professional consistency.

Assessment Coordinators: Coordinate moderation and monitor marking quality.

Moderators: Review assessment instruments and marking decisions objectively.

Digital Platform Administrators: Maintain secure, traceable assessment records and scoring functionality.

Institutional Leadership: Reviews assessment reliability and directs quality improvement.

20. REVIEW AND CONTINUOUS IMPROVEMENT

This document shall be reviewed periodically to reflect:

- Curriculum revisions.

- Teacher feedback.

- Institutional quality assurance findings.

All revisions must be documented through version control.
