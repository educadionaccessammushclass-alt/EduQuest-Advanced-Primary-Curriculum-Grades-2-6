1. PURPOSE

This document establishes a structured system for designing, administering, recording and evaluating learner assessments across Grades 2–6.

It translates the EduQuest Assessment and Mastery Framework into practical grade-level assessment instruments.

The system is designed to:

1. Measure subject knowledge and conceptual understanding.
2. Evaluate reasoning, problem-solving and independent thinking.
3. Assess practical competence and real-world application.
4. Monitor learner progression across Grades 2–6.
5. Identify learning gaps early and guide targeted intervention.
6. establish reliable evidence of mastery.
7. Support inclusive assessment for learners with different learning needs.
8. Provide dependable information for teachers, parents and institutional quality assurance.

Assessment must measure meaningful learning rather than memorisation alone.

2. DESIGN PRINCIPLES

All EduQuest assessment instruments shall follow these principles:

2.1 Curriculum Alignment

Every assessment must correspond to approved curriculum objectives, grade-level learning outcomes and relevant subject competencies.

2.2 Developmental Appropriateness

The complexity of questions, tasks, language and expected independence must increase progressively from Grade 2 to Grade 6.

2.3 Multiple Evidence Sources

No major judgement about learner mastery should depend exclusively on one test or one assessment method.

2.4 Constructive Assessment

Assessment should identify what learners understand, what they can do and what support they require next.

2.5 Authentic Application

Learners should demonstrate their ability to apply knowledge to practical, familiar and progressively unfamiliar situations.

2.6 Fairness and Inclusion

Assessment instruments must avoid unnecessary linguistic, cultural, physical or technological barriers unrelated to the competency being assessed.

2.7 Reliability and Moderation

Assessment tasks and scoring criteria must be sufficiently clear and consistent to support dependable professional judgement.

4. GRADE-LEVEL ASSESSMENT PROGRESSION

4.1 Grade 2 — Foundational Understanding

Assessment emphasis: Recognition, comprehension, basic application and guided demonstration.

Learners should demonstrate the ability to:

- Identify and recognise essential concepts.
- Recall foundational facts and vocabulary.
- Follow simple instructions.
- Explain familiar ideas using simple language.
- Complete guided activities.
- Solve basic problems using familiar methods.
- Demonstrate foundational practical skills.
- Communicate learning through speech, drawing and short written responses.

Recommended instruments:

- Picture-based questions.
- Oral questioning.
- Matching and classification exercises.
- Short written responses.
- Counting and basic calculation tasks.
- Reading and listening comprehension.
- Guided practical demonstrations.
- Teacher observation checklists.
- Short knowledge checks.

Expected assessment conditions: Clear instructions, appropriate visual support, manageable task length and opportunities for oral responses where appropriate.

4.2 Grade 3 — Developing Understanding

Assessment emphasis: Explanation, classification, basic reasoning and increasingly independent application.

Learners should demonstrate the ability to:

- Explain familiar concepts.
- Organise information into categories.
- Identify relationships between ideas.
- Apply learned procedures independently in familiar situations.
- Read and interpret age-appropriate information.
- Solve multi-step problems with appropriate support.
- Present simple findings.
- Reflect on mistakes and corrections.

Recommended instruments:

- Short structured written assessments.
- Reading comprehension exercises.
- Multi-step arithmetic problems.
- Classification and comparison tasks.
- Oral explanations.
- Practical demonstrations.
- Short investigations.
- Guided project tasks.
- Learning journals.

Expected assessment conditions: Gradually reduced scaffolding and increasing opportunities for independent responses.

4.3 Grade 4 — Applied Understanding

Assessment emphasis: Analysis, reasoning, interpretation and practical application.

Learners should demonstrate the ability to:

- Interpret information from different sources.
- Explain processes and relationships.
- Select appropriate methods for solving problems.
- Apply knowledge to unfamiliar but accessible situations.
- Compare evidence and identify differences.
- Conduct simple investigations.
- Organise and communicate findings.
- Demonstrate greater independence in completing tasks.

Recommended instruments:

- Structured and extended-response questions.
- Data interpretation exercises.
- Mathematical problem-solving tasks.
- Scientific investigations.
- Reading and analytical comprehension.
- Practical performance assessments.
- Individual and collaborative projects.
- Short presentations.
- Evidence-based explanations.

Expected assessment conditions: Greater cognitive challenge, less procedural prompting and increasing emphasis on reasoning.

4.4 Grade 5 — Analytical and Independent Application

Assessment emphasis: Analysis, evaluation, multi-step reasoning and independent problem-solving.

Learners should demonstrate the ability to:

- Analyse complex information appropriate to their developmental level.
- Explain the reasoning behind conclusions.
- Compare alternative methods and solutions.
- Interpret tables, diagrams, maps and other representations.
- Apply knowledge across related subjects.
- Conduct planned investigations.
- Evaluate the quality of their own work.
- Communicate structured arguments and findings.
- Complete extended tasks with increasing independence.

Recommended instruments:

- Analytical written assessments.
- Extended problem-solving questions.
- Research and investigation assignments.
- Data interpretation tasks.
- Practical competency assessments.
- Structured debates and presentations.
- Independent projects.
- Reflective reports.
- Portfolio-based assessment.

Expected assessment conditions: Increased independence, evidence-based reasoning and opportunities for interdisciplinary application.

4.5 Grade 6 — Advanced Primary Competence and Transition Readiness

Assessment emphasis: Synthesis, critical reasoning, evaluation, independent application and preparation for secondary education.

Learners should demonstrate the ability to:

- Integrate knowledge from different subject areas.
- Analyse unfamiliar problems systematically.
- Justify conclusions using relevant evidence.
- Evaluate alternative explanations and approaches.
- Design and complete structured investigations.
- Communicate complex ideas clearly.
- Demonstrate independent learning strategies.
- Apply knowledge to practical and community-related situations.
- Reflect critically on performance and learning needs.

Recommended instruments:

- Extended analytical assessments.
- Multi-stage problem-solving tasks.
- Independent research assignments.
- Practical investigations.
- Interdisciplinary projects.
- Evidence-based written arguments.
- Oral presentations and questioning.
- Cumulative assessments.
- Transition-readiness tasks.
- Comprehensive learner portfolios.

Expected assessment conditions: High levels of learner independence, increasingly sophisticated reasoning and clear evidence of readiness for the next stage of education.

6. COGNITIVE DEMAND AND QUESTION DESIGN

Assessment questions should progress from foundational understanding to higher-order thinking.

Cognitive level| Assessment expectation| Example task
Remember| Recall information| Identify, name, list
Understand| Explain meaning| Describe, summarise, classify
Apply| Use knowledge| Calculate, demonstrate, solve
Analyse| Examine relationships| Compare, investigate, distinguish
Evaluate| Make justified judgements| Assess, defend, recommend
Create| Produce original work| Design, construct, develop

These categories are informed by established educational approaches to cognitive progression, including Bloom's Taxonomy.

They are not a rigid sequence that every question must follow. Assessment design must reflect the intended learning outcome and the learner's developmental stage.

Each formal assessment instrument should contain the following information:

1. Assessment identification number.
2. Grade.
3. Subject.
4. Topic or learning unit.
5. Curriculum learning outcomes.
6. Assessment purpose.
7. Assessment method.
8. Instructions to learners.
9. Questions or performance tasks.
10. Marks or scoring criteria.
11. Expected evidence.
12. Assessment duration, where applicable.
13. Accessibility arrangements.
14. Teacher assessment record.
15. Feedback and intervention recommendations.

Every instrument must have an identifiable relationship with the curriculum outcomes it is intended to measure.

8. ASSESSMENT FREQUENCY AND DISTRIBUTION

EduQuest shall use a balanced assessment programme.

8.1 Continuous Assessment

Conducted during teaching and learning to identify understanding, misconceptions and progress.

Examples:

- Oral questions.
- Knowledge checks.
- Class exercises.
- Short written tasks.
- Practical observations.
- Learner reflections.

8.2 Unit Assessment

Conducted after completion of a defined learning unit.

Examples:

- Structured tests.
- Practical tasks.
- Written explanations.
- Problem-solving activities.
- Short projects.

8.3 Periodic Assessment

Conducted at planned intervals to review cumulative learning and identify progression or persistent gaps.

8.4 End-of-Term Assessment

Evaluates achievement across the learning outcomes covered during the term using appropriate combinations of written, oral and practical instruments.

8.5 End-of-Year Assessment

Evaluates cumulative achievement, retention, application and readiness for progression to the next grade.

Assessment frequency and weighting shall be determined through the approved subject and grade assessment plans. No universal weighting shall be assumed without institutional validation.

9. MARKING AND SCORING REQUIREMENTS

All assessment instruments must have clear scoring criteria.

The marking system should distinguish between:

- Accuracy.
- Conceptual understanding.
- Reasoning.
- Method and procedure.
- Application.
- Communication.
- Practical execution.
- Independence.

Where an assessment measures multiple competencies, scoring should reflect those competencies rather than relying exclusively on a single final answer.

9.1 Objective Questions

Use clearly defined correct answers and consistent marking rules.

9.2 Constructed Responses

Use marking guides or rubrics that recognise relevant reasoning, accuracy and quality of explanation.

9.3 Practical Tasks

Use observable performance criteria and structured checklists.

9.4 Projects

Assess planning, research, application, execution, presentation and reflection as appropriate.

All internal score bands and mastery thresholds must be clearly identified as EduQuest institutional standards unless formally adopted through an applicable national framework.

10. MASTERY VERIFICATION

Assessment results must contribute to a broader evidence-based judgement of learner mastery.

The following mastery classifications shall be used in conjunction with the approved EduQuest Assessment and Mastery Framework:

1. Beginning.
2. Developing.
3. Approaching Mastery.
4. Mastered.
5. Advanced Application.

A learner should not be classified as having mastered a competency solely because of one successful test.

Mastery verification should consider:

- Accuracy across relevant tasks.
- Understanding of underlying concepts.
- Consistency of performance.
- Independent application.
- Ability to explain reasoning.
- Transfer to appropriate new situations.
- Retention over time.

Where evidence is insufficient or inconsistent, additional assessment should be conducted.

11. INCLUSIVE ASSESSMENT AND ACCESSIBILITY

Assessment instruments must provide equitable opportunities for learners to demonstrate the intended competencies.

Appropriate arrangements may include:

- Accessible print formats.
- Clear and age-appropriate instructions.
- Additional processing time where justified.
- Oral response alternatives where the assessed competency permits.
- Appropriate assistive technology.
- Enlarged text and accessible layouts.
- Alternative practical materials.
- Reasonable adjustments for documented learning needs.

Adjustments must preserve the construct being assessed. For example, a mathematics assessment may permit an accessible response format without removing the mathematical reasoning requirement.

12. DIGITAL ASSESSMENT IMPLEMENTATION

EduQuest digital assessment systems should support:

1. Grade and subject identification.
2. Learning outcome mapping.
3. Question banks.
4. Multiple question formats.
5. Automatic marking where appropriate.
6. Teacher-reviewed responses.
7. Practical assessment records.
8. Learner progress tracking.
9. Evidence portfolio integration.
10. Mastery status monitoring.
11. Assessment history.
12. Remediation recommendations.
13. Enrichment recommendations.
14. Secure storage and controlled access to learner records.

Digital assessment must not replace professional teacher judgement.

Automated scoring should only be used where the question and response format permit reliable evaluation.

13. QUALITY ASSURANCE AND MODERATION

All formal assessment instruments should undergo quality review before use.

The review should examine:

- Curriculum alignment.
- Accuracy of subject content.
- Clarity of instructions.
- Developmental appropriateness.
- Cognitive demand.
- Cultural and linguistic suitability.
- Accessibility.
- Marking reliability.
- Adequacy of expected evidence.
- Appropriate distribution of question types.

Moderation should be conducted periodically to identify inconsistencies in task difficulty, marking and interpretation of learner performance.

Assessment findings should inform revisions to teaching materials and future instruments.

14. NATIONAL AND INTERNATIONAL ALIGNMENT

EduQuest shall maintain alignment with the applicable Tanzanian curriculum and relevant Tanzania Institute of Education (TIE) syllabi, curriculum guidance and assessment requirements.

The programme may also draw on recognised international educational principles, including:

- Competency-based curriculum design.
- Formative assessment.
- Assessment for learning.
- Constructive alignment.
- Developmental progression.
- Inclusive education.
- Higher-order thinking.
- Evidence-based evaluation.

International frameworks are reference points for quality enhancement. They do not supersede Tanzanian national curriculum requirements.

Any claims of formal national approval, accreditation or equivalence must be supported by the appropriate official evidence.

15. GOVERNANCE AND RESPONSIBILITY

Curriculum leadership shall approve the assessment architecture and ensure curriculum alignment.

Subject specialists shall develop and review subject-specific instruments.

Teachers shall administer assessments, record evidence, provide feedback and recommend interventions.

Assessment coordinators shall oversee assessment schedules, moderation and reporting.

Digital platform administrators shall maintain the integrity, security and accessibility of assessment records.

Institutional leadership shall review assessment quality and use findings to guide programme improvement.
