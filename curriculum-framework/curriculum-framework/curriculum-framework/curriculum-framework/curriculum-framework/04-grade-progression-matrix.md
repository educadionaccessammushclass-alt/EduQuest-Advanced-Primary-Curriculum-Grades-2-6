## 1. PURPOSE

This document establishes the developmental and academic progression framework governing learning outcomes across Grades 2–6.

It ensures that knowledge, skills, competencies and intellectual demands develop systematically across the five grades.

The matrix shall guide subject curriculum development, lesson sequencing, assessment design and learner progression.

## 2. PROGRESSION PRINCIPLES

All subject development shall observe the following principles:

1. Learning builds upon previously acquired knowledge.
2. Foundational competencies must be secured before advanced application.
3. Complexity increases progressively.
4. Learners move from concrete experiences towards abstract reasoning.
5. Guided learning gradually develops into independent performance.
6. Knowledge must transfer across subjects and real-world contexts.
7. Assessment evidence informs instructional decisions.
8. Advanced learners receive appropriate enrichment opportunities.

## 3. GENERAL DEVELOPMENTAL PROGRESSION

### GRADE 2 — FOUNDATIONAL DEVELOPMENT

Primary emphasis:

- Recognition and understanding.
- Basic reading and writing.
- Number sense.
- Concrete mathematical operations.
- Observation and description.
- Listening and speaking.
- Classification and comparison.
- Simple practical activities.

Expected learner development:

The learner demonstrates foundational knowledge, communicates simple ideas and completes structured learning activities with appropriate support.

### GRADE 3 — KNOWLEDGE EXPANSION

Primary emphasis:

- Reading comprehension.
- Extended written expression.
- Mathematical operations.
- Pattern recognition.
- Guided scientific investigation.
- Classification and interpretation.
- Structured problem-solving.
- Independent practice.

Expected learner development:

The learner explains concepts, identifies relationships and applies acquired knowledge to familiar situations.

### GRADE 4 — CONCEPTUAL DEVELOPMENT

Primary emphasis:

- Deeper subject understanding.
- Mathematical reasoning.
- Interpretation of information.
- Scientific enquiry.
- Structured analytical thinking.
- Independent written communication.
- Evidence-based explanations.
- Multi-step activities.

Expected learner development:

The learner analyses information, applies concepts independently and provides logical explanations.

### GRADE 5 — ANALYTICAL AND APPLIED COMPETENCE

Primary emphasis:

- Advanced primary-level subject knowledge.
- Multi-step problem-solving.
- Extended reading and writing.
- Independent investigation.
- Data interpretation.
- Practical projects.
- Comparative reasoning.
- Collaborative learning.

Expected learner development:

The learner investigates questions, evaluates alternatives and produces solutions supported by relevant evidence.

### GRADE 6 — SYNTHESIS AND SECONDARY READINESS

Primary emphasis:

- Consolidated subject mastery.
- Independent research.
- Complex problem-solving.
- Interpretation and evaluation of evidence.
- Mathematical and scientific reasoning.
- Extended communication.
- Interdisciplinary application.
- Preparation for secondary education.

Expected learner development:

The learner integrates knowledge, justifies conclusions, solves unfamiliar problems and demonstrates readiness for more advanced academic study.

## 5. CROSS-CURRICULAR COMPETENCY PROGRESSION

### Critical Thinking

Grade 2: Identify and describe.

Grade 3: Compare and explain.

Grade 4: Analyse and interpret.

Grade 5: Evaluate and justify.

Grade 6: Synthesise and defend conclusions.

### Creativity

Grade 2: Produce simple original work.

Grade 3: Develop ideas using guidance.

Grade 4: Design solutions to defined problems.

Grade 5: Develop and improve independent projects.

Grade 6: Produce and present original solutions.

### Research and Inquiry

Grade 2: Observe and ask questions.

Grade 3: Collect information with guidance.

Grade 4: Investigate and organise information.

Grade 5: Compare evidence and explain findings.

Grade 6: Conduct structured independent investigations.

### Communication

Grade 2: Express basic ideas.

Grade 3: Explain information clearly.

Grade 4: Organise and present ideas.

Grade 5: Communicate evidence-based arguments.

Grade 6: Present and defend reasoned conclusions.

### Digital Competence

Grade 2: Recognise and use appropriate digital tools with supervision.

Grade 3: Complete guided digital learning activities.

Grade 4: Organise information and demonstrate responsible use.

Grade 5: Use digital tools for research and creative production.

Grade 6: Apply digital tools critically, safely and independently at an age-appropriate level.

## 6. LEARNER PROGRESSION AND SUPPORT

Learners shall not be classified solely by age or grade placement.

Progression decisions should consider:

- Demonstrated knowledge.
- Conceptual understanding.
- Practical competence.
- Independent performance.
- Assessment evidence.
- Identified learning gaps.

Remedial support, differentiated instruction and enrichment shall be available across all grades.

## 7. SECONDARY READINESS

By completion of Grade 6, learners should demonstrate:

1. Functional literacy and strong reading comprehension.
2. Secure mathematical foundations.
3. Scientific reasoning.
4. Effective written and oral communication.
5. Independent learning habits.
6. Digital responsibility.
7. Research and information-handling skills.
8. Problem-solving competence.
9. Ethical awareness and responsible citizenship.
10. Ability to apply knowledge in unfamiliar contexts.

These outcomes shall support transition to the next educational stage without misrepresenting official secondary-school admission requirements.

## 8. CURRICULUM VALIDATION

The progression matrix shall undergo subject-specific validation against:

- Applicable TIE syllabi.
- Grade-level learning outcomes.
- Recognised international progression frameworks.
- Learner developmental expectations.
- Assessment evidence.

Any subject content that is advanced, deferred or enriched shall be documented and justified.

## 9. REVIEW REQUIREMENTS

The matrix shall be reviewed whenever:

- National curriculum requirements change.
- Subject specifications are revised.
- Assessment evidence identifies progression gaps.
- Academic review identifies inappropriate sequencing.
- New research informs developmental or pedagogical practice.
